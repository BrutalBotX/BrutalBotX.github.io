<h1 class="zw-paragraph heading0" style="text-align:center;">#205</h1>

<hr>
<p>Is there any middle-aged man who would look so would look so out of place with his body leaning against the back gate, gazing out at the field?</p>
<p>Both externally and internally, he does not fit the mature look of a father.</p>

<p>I sigh at my own father's appearance, which makes it seem that the relationship between father and son is not that great.</p>
<p>
</p><p>I was even more cold-hearted than usual since he had returned to Japan without prior notice.</p>

<p>"What are you doing here...?"</p>

<p>"Minato... were you here?"</p>

<p>"This is the high school I go to..."</p>
<p>
</p><p>What's this...?</p>
<p>The only reason why this person would come to a place like this is because his son goes there.</p>
<p>Even if he tries to look cool and narrow his eyes and let out a sorrowful voice, it's my father who's saying it, so I don't think anything of it.</p>

<p>I just want him to tell me what he needs and send him on his way.</p>
<p>
</p><p>My father gives me a disapproving look, then quickly drops his gaze to my hand.</p>
<p>All I have in my hand is a bag of rice balls from the school cafeteria.</p>

<p>I'm too old to be so picky.</p>

<p>"Kaede has a gymnastics festival too... so she didn't have time to make bento."</p>

<p>Like me, Kaede, who had Sports festival in the opposite direction, went to school earlier than usual.<br>As a brother, I felt bad asking her to prepare lunch for two on such a day.</p>
<p>However, that didn't mean that telling the person in front of me would make any difference, so I just told him the truth.</p>

<p>"It's from your mother, I've come to deliver it."</p>

<p>He took out a bento from his bag on his shoulder.</p>
<p>The two-tiered lunch box, wrapped in a cute Japanese wrapping cloth, was the same one I'd been using since junior high school.</p>

<p>"Mom, you made it? I wouldn't have bought this if you'd have contacted me..."</p>

<p>With that, I held up the bag hanging in my hand.</p>
<p>It doesn't matter because I'll eat when I'm hungry after the Sports festival is over, but I could have done that without reducing the burden on my allowance.</p>

<p>I turned my gaze towards him with such intent.</p>

<p>However, I'm happy to see that I had the mindset that I would miss having onigiri from the school cafeteria at a big school event.</p>
<p>The reason why I don't see my mom behind my dad is probably because she is having lunch with Kaede.</p>
<p>It was my sister's first Sports event, so it would be better for her to have lunch with her parents.</p>

<p>"So you're here as the courier guy, thanks."</p>

<p>Turning my body to the side, I thanked him briefly and started to walk to the shade of the tree where I wanted to go.</p>
<p>But, it was my father's small voice that stopped me from advancing.</p>

<p>"He didn't seem to be bothering Shizuku-chan and the others."</p>

<p>"..."</p>

<p>I turn around and started to search for the meaning of his words.</p>
<p>My father's eyes were not on me, but on Shizuku and Yuuto, who were having lunch with their classmates in a corner of the field.</p>

<p>I wonder what he's referring to...</p>
<p>If I remember correctly, I feel like I've done nothing but cause trouble for these girls.</p>
<p>The words came out of embarrassment, unlike these thoughts.</p>

<p>"...You've been watching the three-legged race?"</p>

<p>In terms of time, the clearest thing my father would've seen must have been the three-legged race.</p>
<p>In other words, he also saw me get emotional and thrust my fist into the air.</p>

<p>Shame welled up in me, and I felt a slight heat rise in my body.</p>
<p>It is more embarrassing to be seen by parents than by fellow students.</p>
<p>Dad, perhaps realizing this, lets a smile slip from his mouth.</p>


<p>"Even I would show up at my kid's event."</p>

<p>"..."</p>

<p>The words out of my father's mouth are so parental, so proud, that I can't help but feel a little embarrassed.</p>

<p>The image of the person in front of me, who had told me so parenthetically and proudly, was a far cry from the image of my father that I remembered.</p>
<p>I had been to elementary school, middle school, and high school, but all I could remember was my mother and sister.</p>

<p>I don't see my father next to them.</p>
<p>I attended both the junior high graduation ceremony and the high school entrance ceremony, but never once did my father voluntarily announce his presence at any of them.</p>

<p>I noticed that only one person disappeared, and before I knew it, this person was at home.</p>
<p>I can count the number of times he was in a club and the number of times he came to watch a game.</p>

<p>So it was a surprise that he showed up here again today.</p>


<p>"That's not something that you would say..."</p>

<p>I said, turning my body to face him again, having once turned my back on him.</p>
<p>I felt annoyed at his son's reaction and the way he just stared at him, without any sign of anger.</p>

<p>However, I never put into words the irritation I feel.<br>...I've gotten used to this irritation.</p>

<p>My father doesn't talk to me much, probably because he feels like he's looking at his old self.</p>
<p>The old me, with no strengths, weaknesses, or hidden talents.</p>


<p>"Hurry up and get back to Kaede, ...you should be watching your kid's big day."</p>

<p>You're going to see your child's big day, right?</p>
<p>You should leave me alone and return as soon as possible rather than miss it and regret it.</p>

<p>"I'm leaving Japan on the last flight today."</p>

<p>After a short speech, my father turns on his heel and returns to the car parked behind him.</p>
<p>On the way back to the car, he looked back at me once.</p>
<p>He may have something left to say, but he just stood there and said just one sentence.</p>

<p>"The two of you will get along well..."</p>

<p>"I see."</p>

<p>Satisfied with my comment, he gets into his car and proceeds down the narrow road.</p>
<p>He turned left at the intersection just after proceeding through the back gate, and an involuntary sigh escaped my lips as I looked away from him until he was out of sight.</p>

<p>"What did I expect...?"</p>

<p>I muttered to myself, but the words fizzled out, drowned out by the sounds around me.</p>
<p>Even though I knew in my head that it was impossible, a faint hope swirled in my mind that maybe he had come to see me.</p>
<p>I looked up and stood there looking up at the bright blue sky.</p>
<p>
</p>
<p>"You're glad that you don't need your father to see the rest of it, right?"</p>

<p>From a short distance away, a familiar voice reached my ears, followed by the sound of sand being stepped on.</p>
<p>I didn't even bother to turn around to hear the words.</p>

<p>"I'm no longer in the events, you know."</p>

<p>"That's not why I'm here."</p>

<p>President, who walked up to me with her long chestnut hair fluttering, stopped next to me and turned her eyes in pursuit of the figure that was already out of sight.</p>
<p>There is no meaning, no return, just an uninhabited road stretching out before us.</p>

<p>"There may be another Sports Festival next year, but this year may be the last one you will build with these girls."</p>

<p>I don't need to be told who the girls are that President mentioned in her words.</p>
<p>But that is only a trivial issue for that person.</p>

<p>"I'm not the kind of person who cares about such things."</p>

<p>When I gave a wry smile akin to resignation, President looked a little sad.</p>
<p>For the President, this is her last sports festival.</p>
<p>It is inevitable that she would worry about the parent-child situation of the person with whom she has tentatively made an arrangement.</p>
<p></p>
<p>However, this is a matter for the Shinra family and not for President to worry about.</p>
<p>To reassure her, I lightly stretch my body and make my usual unmotivated expression.</p>

<p>President, as expected, does not interject any further and gently brushes away her long hair as if to shake off her thoughts.</p>

<p>"I see... so Shinra, come and have lunch with me."</p>

<p>"...I don't like hot places."</p>

<p>When I reply, the president puts a smile on her mouth and walks toward the student council tent.</p>
<p>I walked with her behind me, and after turning around like my father for just a moment, I realized that the behavior was similar, and in my heart I could not help but feel irritated.</p>
