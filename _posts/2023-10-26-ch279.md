<h1 style="text-align: center"><strong>#279</strong></h1>
<hr />
<p>The new year began with a quiet dawn, not a dramatic one.</p>
<p>Putting on the school uniform after a long while, I slung my bag over my shoulder and headed for the front door.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>At the entrance, there was a photo frame displayed.</p>
<p>I had shown the photo to my mother and sister and they liked it, so they decided to display it here so they could see it every day.</p>
<p>I turned my gaze to the photo.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>A boy with a radiant smile and a girl casting a sidelong glance at him. Three others were standing on either side, and the two girls held their gifts with care, gazing at the camera.</p>
<p>In the center, the boy sat. Looking at it now, I still noticed a certain stiffness in my expression.</p>
<p>My smile was a bit awkward, but it wasn’t fake. It was the clumsiness of a genuine expression that only I could discern, being my own face.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>I had to feel a bit embarrassed looking at this photo every day. But since I also liked the photo itself, I accepted the current situation without complaint.</p>
<p>As I finished changing into my outdoor shoes at the entrance, I glanced at the photo for a moment, reflecting on all this.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Alright, I’m off.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Take care. Mama will be so lonely without Minato-chan that she might die, so come back home early after school.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>My mom waved, and she did it so naturally, unashamedly, and with that childish crying gesture. I closed the front door without acknowledging her last words.</p>
<p>The New Year’s wind was still cold. I gently pulled my scarf up close to my mouth.</p>
<p>When I reached the road, I saw some familiar faces already looking at me.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>There were four of them. Seeing them, I couldn’t help but let out a slight smile.</p>
<p>Confirming their presence, I walked closer to them.</p>
<p>…You guys, aren’t your houses in the opposite direction from mine?”</p>
<p>Naturally, I found it suspicious, but I believed that in cases like these, it was a golden rule not to question them.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>No, it’s definitely double effort, isn’t it?”</p>
<p>Thinking such a thing, I walked up to them, marking the first day of the new school year together.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>And so, the final chapter, with the third term began.</p>
<p> </p>
