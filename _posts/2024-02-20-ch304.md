<h1 style="text-align: center">#304</h1>
<p> </p>
<hr />
<p>On the TV installed in the AV room, footage of past farewell parties was playing.</p>
<p>After gathering the DVDs for reference materials on the desk and arranging them neatly, I found myself alone in the deserted room after school, staring at the screen in front of me.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Whether it was last year, the year before, or even several years ago, the farewells for the graduating seniors followed a similar format.</p>
<p>Routine, tradition, those words came to mind.</p>
<p>It’s not necessarily a bad thing; sticking to a single theme allows us to allocate resources more efficiently, resulting in a simple yet high-quality event.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>In a sense, it’s the established form.</p>
<p>Considering this, I can easily imagine the format of past farewell parties.</p>
<p>First, the third-year students enter the gymnasium where the entire school is assembled.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>The vice principal delivers an opening speech, followed by a congratulatory address from the school principal. Since the principal will also deliver a speech at the graduation ceremony, this one is kept brief.</p>
<p>Since the farewell party is the last chance for third-year students and current students to meet, the teachers keep their remarks to a minimum.</p>
<p>Then, a representative from the second-year students delivers words of celebration, followed by a message from the third-year students to the current students.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Since Koizumi has been entrusted with delivering the student representative speech at the graduation ceremony, the role of representative in this case is not limited to the student council president.</p>
<p>Finally, bouquets are presented to the third-year students, and that concludes the proceedings.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>It’s simple and neither particularly good nor bad.</p>
<p>Both the senders and the recipients seem satisfied, achieving a passing grade, so to speak.</p>
<p>In fact, attempting to change things might risk making a negative impression.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>From a different perspective, it’s called tradition because it garners a certain amount of approval.</p>
<p>This time around, there’s no need for anything outlandish.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>That’s exactly why it’s difficult.</p>
<p>Even if we were to dance or perform a comedy routine, if what’s required is not laughter but memories, it would be counterproductive.</p>
<p>Do I lack knowledge? Creativity? Time?</p>
<p>Do I need something closer to the spontaneous ideas of students who live for the moment, like those carefree students who would dismiss this as unnecessary?</p>
<p> </p>
<p>As I pondered, only the risks associated with the ideas that came to mind flashed through my mind.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“What’s up? Have you found any good ideas?”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“…Not at all. This is a situation where I really need more manpower.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Hino-kun, entering the AV room, immediately posed this question.</p>
<p>He was carrying a mountain of materials in his hands.</p>
<p>In the AV room, where mainly videos are stored, Hino-kun was in charge of transporting materials to the staff room, where past photos and other items are kept.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>As he spread out the photos on the desk, one by one, it became apparent that scenes deviating from the norm, like those in the videos, were not captured.</p>
<p>As Hino-kun looked over the footage, I realized the same as I had seen before.</p>
<p>Then, he raised a question.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Are these students wearing armbands the current students who volunteered for the farewell party?”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Probably. They’re different from the ones the student council wears, and I’ve seen a few students wearing them in other footage… I think the teachers can bring them if we ask.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>The orange armbands are not used by the student council. They were likely provided at some point to make it easier for the volunteers to move around.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>…Since I might be wandering around the school for a while, it might be a good idea to have one just in case.</p>
<p>I’ll keep it in the back of my mind. In the meantime, since no ideas are coming to mind as I search through the materials, it wouldn’t hurt to make progress on small tasks.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Arranging the student layout in the gymnasium, calculating and acquiring supplies… There’s plenty to do even with a small number of people.</p>
<p>For now, let’s proceed with the detailed work while gathering people and compiling ideas… That seems to be the only option at the moment.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Despite the issues with numbers and ideas, let’s focus on what we can do now… Fortunately, Koizumi said I only need to show up at the student council after school, so it’s easy to move around.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Yeah, I’m fine with not having much to do in the student council…”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Let’s start by going to the gymnasium to confirm the layout for the day.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“I didn’t hear anything…”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Picking up the necessary materials, I stopped the video that was playing and got up from my seat.</p>
<p>Since we need to lock the AV room door before leaving, I let Hino-kun go ahead.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>As I closed and locked the door, I heard a voice from the left.</p>
<p>Turning around, I saw Kirasaka standing there, brushing back her slightly longer black hair.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“So, it’s true that Hino-kun is the only participating student?”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Kirasaka… You’re still at school?”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“I was looking for interesting books in the library. But really, with only two weeks left until the farewell party, can you two handle it?”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Kirasaka looked at Hino-kun, who was standing just behind me, with a mix of curiosity and concern evident in her voice.</p>
<p>Perhaps she had come to check on us before leaving.</p>
<p>I tightly shut my slightly opened mouth in response to her seemingly intriguing yet worrisome tone.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Having only me and Hino-kun seems like a severe lack of manpower… I almost said the usual excuse, but I couldn’t make excuses. I had decided to participate myself, despite the unexpected situation of student participation.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>From Kirasaka’s perspective, it’s hard to believe that we’ll be able to pull it off even if I said we would.</p>
<p>Perhaps she came to lend a hand.</p>
<p>But that wouldn’t make much sense.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Oh, sorry to bother. I’ll head to the gym first.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“My bad… I’ll catch up soon.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Looking uncomfortable, Hino-kun bowed slightly to Kirasaka before hurrying off towards the gym.</p>
<p>Since there was nothing to do and no atmosphere for casual conversation, he probably wanted to leave quickly.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>As I watched his back for a few seconds before turning my gaze back, Kirasaka, who had taken a step forward, came into view.</p>
<p>She peered into my eyes, as if trying to discern my intentions, causing me to instinctively avert my gaze.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“I’ll help too. Akane-san is included in the graduating class, after all.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Kirasaka smiled gently and extended her right hand.</p>
<p>Her expression conveyed kindness towards the offered hand.</p>
<p>Without realizing it, I started to reach out with my right arm, but kept it by my side and shook my head.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“Nah… This is something I have to do on my own. It wouldn’t mean anything if I didn’t finish it without relying on you or Shizuku.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“…Was that something someone told you?”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“No, it’s not… It’s something I thought of myself.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>At her inquiry, I responded promptly.</p>
<p>Kirasaka narrowed her eyes at my words.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a convincing denial like a hermit’s denial without explaining the reason.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>From the perspective of someone who had extended their hand to help a friend, being rejected could evoke discomfort.</p>
<p>My actions were just empty boasts when put into words.</p>
<p>Just empty words of idealism for their sake and for our future.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>“It’s not like you, Minato-kun… We’re more than willing to help you.”</p>
<p> </p>
<p>From Kirasaka’s perspective, I must be taking on an impossible task.</p>
<p>In reality, I couldn’t imagine successfully completing it at all.</p>
<p>And then, bearing the responsibility of running the farewell party as the representative of the current students.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>I could easily envision the reproach from the students.</p>
<p>A sad smile on her face, Kirasaka turned back and disappeared around the corner of the hallway, but not before smiling once more.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Was it because of my past actions?</p>
<p>Lately, it felt like my own inadequacies and apologies to those around me had accumulated like a weight on my shoulders.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Whether the outcome waiting for me would be fortunate or unfortunate.</p>
<p>Regardless, I couldn’t allow myself to stop this path that I had already started.</p>
<p> </p>
