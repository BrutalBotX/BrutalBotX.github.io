<h1 class="zw-paragraph heading0" style="text-align:center;">#172</h1>

<hr>
<p><span class="EOP">&nbsp;</span>Once a year, students flock to the plaza in front of the main gate, where they only gather on the first day of the new school term.</p>
<p>There, a bulletin board is set up where information regarding the school gets displayed.</p>

<p>The reason why so many students are crowded around the bulletin board is probably because the information that has just been posted was unexpected for them.</p>

<p>Sakuranaoka Academy's Student Council Election.<br>At this very moment, the candidates running for the Student Council President election, the first round of the election, were being announced to the us.</p>
<p>An unexpected confrontation, which until today had only been disclosed to the Student Council members and a few others involved with them, was about to begin.</p>

<p>Facing Koizumi, the current Student Council Vice President, is Shiraishi, a promising first-year student.</p>
<p>Since everyone had assumed that Koizumi would take over as the next student council president, this information caused quite a stir in the school.</p>

<p>"..."</p>

<p>I also went to the bulletin board in front of the main gate and watched the scene from a more familiar place. If I had to describe that scene in one word, it would be shocking.</p>
<p>But the reactions differed from grade to grade more than I had imagined.</p>
<p>I guess it would be right to say that the senpais seemed more surprised and the kouhais looked at it expectantly.</p>
<p>Not to go so far as to call it a stereotype, but the senpais were probably more shocked because they had assumed that Koizumi would inevitably be the president.</p>

<p>However, they were said surprised a bit differently as if it were someone else's problem.</p>

<p>The photos of the two candidates were boldly displayed on the wall, but at this point, there appeared to be a big difference between the two.<br>To put it simply, there is a big difference, look-wise.</p>

<p>Koizumi looked more nervous than ever, and his photo were full of a sense of seriousness, as usual.<br>In contrast, Shiraishi was photographed from a calculated angle that allowed her to make the most of her charm.</p>

<p>There are a lot of photos floating around these days that are concerned about looking good on SNS, but this one is completely outdone even after being taken from a Polaroid camera... But our vice president Koizumi...<br>With the student council room in the background, he stood straight with his neat uniform.</p>
<p>For better or worse, his expression was stiff and serious.</p>

<p>If it were a formal election, Koizumi's serious impression would be a plus, but in an environment that consisted of students and on the top of it was their sense of fun, it could not be considered effective.</p>

<p>In this respect, Shiraishi's photograph would not give students a bad impression.<br>The lawn in the background, combined with the sunlight, gave a cheerful impression.</p>
<p>
</p><p>Her smiling face should probably be a part of her calculations, but it was hard to tell.<br>The students must have had a similar impression to mine, and I think many of them favored Shiraishi for the impression in the photo.</p>

<p>"I wonder who that is?"</p>

<p>Kirasaka muttered, looking at the photo of Shiraishi.</p>

<p>"For some reason, after seeing Shiraishi's true nature, it feels really deliberate."</p>

<p>Said Shizuku, standing across from me.</p>
<p>...Yeah.</p>

<p>The Shiraishi we've seen all through our summer vacation is a different person.</p>
<p>The picture is so perfect that it oozes out those impressions.</p>

<p>Unlike those around me, I looked at the bulletin board with a wry smile.<br class="zw-br">Below it was a small description for the next student council members.</p>

<p>[Candidates for the next student council officers should come to the student council office this week.]</p>
<p>That sentence was written in an understated manner.</p>
<p></p>
<p>I don't know if Hino-kun will still be on the Student Council next year, but even so, with the president leaving, there should be a shortage of people.</p>
<p>They probably want to reinforce the council not only from first-year students but also from second-year students.</p>

<p>"Minato-kun, aren't you going to participate in the election?"</p>

<p>This was a question that had been asked many times by Shizuku.<br>No matter how many times it is asked, the answer will never change.</p>

<p>"...No comment."</p>

<p>If there was a possibility that I would remain on the student council, it would be if Shiraishi became vice president and Koizumi became president.<br>But it's not very likely to happen.</p>

<p>If Shiraishi is elected president, she will tend to focus on people with abilities.</p>
<p>Naturally, her ideal student council officers would be expected to have the appropriate abilities.</p>

<p>In other words, there is no place for a student like me who has nothing special to offer.</p>
<p>Koizumi is no exception.</p>

<p>However, I have spent a little time in the same surroundings with him as a member.</p>
<p>Even if you call it sympathy, but I think the only possibility left that I could continue to be a member.<br>Before that, unless I have a change of heart, it would be unthinkable for me even to think about it.</p>

<p>Having seen what we wanted to see, we turned on our heels and headed back inside the school.<br>On the way, I saw many students running in the opposite direction we were going, toward the bulletin board, and they all had smiles on their faces.</p>

<p>I wondered if the new school term and the student council election had made them excited.<br>As I was walking along the pathway leading to the schoolyard, I saw two figures.</p>
<p>They were each wearing a sash and each was addressing a group of students.</p>

<p>"Nice to meet you!"</p>

<p>"I am Momiji Shiraishi, hello."</p>

<p>Seeing the two of them, I realized what was happening.<br>The whole ruckus about the student council elections that had begun before the summer break was now in full swing.</p>

<p>''Do you have any plans to help Koizumi or Shiraishi-san?''</p>

<p>Yuuto said with his arms folded behind his head, seemingly without much interest.<br>I shook my head in response.</p>

<p>If they have something to discuss with me or something they need me to do, that's fine, but there's no need for me to come forward and help them.<br>They would think about it, and if they needed help, they would ask for it.</p>

<p>It can also be considered a nuisance, depending on the situation, to come forward and help.</p>
<p>I don't want to be seen as intruding.</p>

<p>"Well, I'll just go say hello."</p>

<p>But, we aren't strangers.<br>I can't pick sides, but I can at least call out to the two of them as a show of support.</p>

<p>When I told the three of them in that sense, all three of them turned to me with smirks and expressions that made it hard for me to hold back my grin.</p>

<p>"You're surprisingly good at taking care of people, Shinra-kun."</p>

<p>"...Shut up."</p>


<p>With some embarrassment, I walked over to the two who were working hard a little further ahead of me, just as I realized the reason for their smiles.<br>If this student council election is fought by themselves instead of relying on someone else, it would end with no regrets for the two of them.</p>

<p>President had hoped so too, and I wondered if Shiraishi could pick up even a sliver of that when she saw Koizumi on her summer vacation.</p>
<p>That what was I wondered it as I walked ahead.</p>
